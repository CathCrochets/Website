Looking after it

Handmade crochet lasts for years if you are a bit kind to it. None of this is complicated, and most of it comes down to warm water, no wringing, and drying flat.

Washing

Hand wash if you can

Most of my things would survive a machine. I still would not put them in one. Five minutes in the sink beats explaining to yourself why the hat is now a different shape.

By hand, which takes about five minutes

  1. Lukewarm water, a drop of mild detergent

    Wool wash or a gentle liquid. Not boiling, not biological, and nothing with bleach in it.

  2. Swirl, do not scrub

    Move it around gently and press it under the water. Rubbing crochet against itself is what makes it felt and bobble.

  3. Rinse in clean water

    Same temperature. A sudden cold rinse after warm water is not kind to the fibres.

  4. Press, never wring

    Lay it on a dry towel, roll the towel up and press. Twisting it out is the single fastest way to ruin the shape.

If you really are going to use the machine. Coolest wash you have, delicate or hand wash cycle, inside a mesh laundry bag, lowest spin. Never a tumble dryer. Wooden buttons do not enjoy any of this, so if a piece has one, hand wash it.

Cotton things are tougher

Coasters and bookmarks are cotton, not the soft chenille, so they take a lot more. Cool machine wash in a bag is fine and they will come out looking new. Coasters do stain eventually, mind. That is what they are there for.

Cases and sleeves

Take the Kindle, the Tamagotchi or the Switch out first. Obvious, but worth saying. And let the case dry all the way through before the device goes back in, because crochet holds damp for longer than it looks.

Drying and storing

Flat, and out of the sun

Dry it flat

On a towel or a rack, patted back into the shape you want it to keep. While it is damp you can nudge it about and it will set that way, which is handy if a hat has gone a bit oval in the wash.

Nothing hot, nothing bright

Radiators cook the fibres and strong sun fades the colours, particularly the pinks and the deep red. A normal room and a bit of patience is all it needs.

Fold, do not hang

Crochet has weight and it will stretch itself out of shape on a hook or a hanger over time. A drawer or a shelf is better, and do not seal it in a plastic bag for months.

The normal wear and tear

Snags, bobbles and shedding

A pulled loop

Do not cut it. This is the important one. Every stitch in a crochet piece is connected to the next, so one snip can unravel a whole row.

Instead, work the pulled loop back in with your fingers or the blunt end of a needle, easing the slack along the row a bit at a time until it evens out. It takes a minute and it will disappear.

Bobbling and fluff

The soft chenille sheds a little at first. That settles down after a wash or two and is not a fault.

Light bobbling on a well used piece is normal for any soft yarn. A fabric shaver on its lowest setting sorts it, or just pick them off by hand. Do not attack it with a razor.

Keeping the animals safe

I make dress up things, and dress up things need supervision. None of this is meant to put you off, it is just the stuff I would want somebody to tell me.

While it is on

  • Stay in the room. Hats and collars are for photographs and for the afternoon, not for leaving on.
  • Tie the ties loosely enough to get a finger underneath, and trim nothing, the length is there so it can be tied comfortably.
  • Watch how they are with it. Flattened ears, freezing on the spot or trying to back out of it all mean take it off.
  • Take it off before they eat, sleep, go outside or are left on their own.

Things I would never do

  • Use a crochet collar as a real collar. There is no quick release buckle and it will not break away if it catches on something.
  • Clip a lead to one. It is decorative and it is not built to take a pull.
  • Let a cat chew or play with it. Cats swallow yarn and string, and that turns into an emergency vet visit very quickly.
  • Force it on. If they have decided against it today, they have decided. Lola gets a treat and a lie down and we try again another time.

Store them somewhere the animals cannot get at them when they are not being worn.

If something goes wrong

I would rather mend it than have you bin it

If a seam gives, a button comes off or something comes apart in a way it obviously should not have, send me a photo. I made it, so I know how it goes back together, and in most cases I would rather fix it than have it end up in a drawer.
